# Design Note #004 — Stop Asking AI for Marketing Tactics: How to Break a Side-Business Bottleneck with a Problem-First Framework

## Overview

This Design Note documents a problem-first business diagnosis workflow. It begins by clarifying the decision problem and structural constraints before considering interventions.

The case study describes a friend's wedding-planning and emcee side business. She had positive word-of-mouth and steady referrals, but faced inconsistent projects and lengthy client conversations alongside a demanding full-time job.

## Problem

The initial concern was that the side business felt stuck. The diagnosis reframed the question from attracting more weddings to reducing the business's dependence on one person's available hours.

The diagnostic exercise surfaced three interpretations: revenue was closely tied to personal service time, referrals offered limited direct control over customer acquisition, and professional knowledge had not been organized into reusable assets. The article explicitly describes these as interpretations based on the case description, not audited business findings.

## Workflow

1. Diagnose structural constraints before generating marketing tactics.
2. Identify existing expertise and assets, such as scripts, templates, checklists, and procedures.
3. Use progressively narrower prompts to explore service packages and acquisition options.
4. Arrange the proposal in sequence: document expertise, standardize workflows, test a small digital-product MVP, build SEO-oriented content, and consider education or consulting later.
5. Have a human evaluate and discuss the proposed path; validate any actual business impact in the market.

## Human-AI Collaboration

### Human

- Supplies the situation and chooses the next questions.
- Evaluates the internal logic of AI-generated diagnoses and options.
- Brings the proposed pathway into a real conversation and decides what to test.

### AI

- Assists with diagnosis and option generation.
- Helps surface potential constraints and organize a logical sequence of options.
- Does not verify the business findings or establish that a proposed plan has been implemented.

## Limitations

The case study documents a proposal and a discussion, not implementation results or audited business findings. Suggested time windows and an illustrative NT$299–499 product price were not validated through a reported launch or real-world sales experiment. Any business impact must be tested in the market.
