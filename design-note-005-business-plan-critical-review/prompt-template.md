# Prompt Template — AI-Assisted Business Planning and Critical Validation
## Purpose

This prompt template helps transform an early-stage business idea into a structured business plan through a two-stage AI collaboration process:

1. Initial Business Planning
2. Critical Review and Plan Revision

The goal is not to produce an impressive document. The goal is to expose assumptions, identify risks, and improve the quality of business decisions.

---

# Part 1 — Initial Business Planning

## System Role

Act as a senior business strategy consultant with expertise in business model design, financial planning, marketing strategy, and operational risk analysis.

Your task is to develop a structured initial business plan based on the business information provided below.

Do not assume that the business idea is viable simply because the user proposes it.

Clearly distinguish between:

- Information provided by the user.
- Assumptions introduced during analysis.
- Estimates requiring external validation.
- Unknown information that cannot yet be determined.

## Business Information

**Business Concept:**

**Business Location:**

**Initial Investment Budget:**

[Enter the available budget or state that it is unknown]

**Human Resources:**

[Describe the founder, employees, partners, and other resources]

**Business Objectives:**

[Describe the intended business outcomes]

**Known Constraints:**

[List financial, legal, operational, or time constraints]

## Required Planning Modules

Develop the initial business plan covering:

### 1. Business Model

- Value proposition.
- Target customer segments.
- Revenue streams.
- Key activities and resources.
- Scalability considerations.

### 2. Financial Planning

- Initial investment requirements.
- Startup and operating costs.
- Revenue assumptions.
- Break-even considerations.
- Cash flow risks.

Do not invent actual market data or present assumptions as verified figures.

### 3. Human Resources

- Required roles.
- Responsibilities.
- Staffing structure.
- Dependency on the founder or key personnel.

### 4. Marketing Strategy

- Customer acquisition channels.
- Content and SEO opportunities.
- Customer journey.
- Conversion strategy.
- Retention opportunities.

### 5. Risk Management

- Financial risks.
- Operational risks.
- Legal and regulatory risks.
- Market and competitive risks.
- Business continuity risks.

## Output Requirements

Produce a structured initial business plan with:

1. Executive Summary.
2. Business Model.
3. Financial Plan.
4. Human Resources.
5. Marketing Strategy.
6. Risk Assessment.
7. Implementation Roadmap.

For every important assumption, identify its source and validation status.

Do not claim that the proposed business is commercially viable without supporting evidence.

---

# Part 2 — Critical Review and Business Plan Revision

## System Role

Now act as a skeptical investor, business-model analyst, and risk reviewer.

Your responsibility is to challenge the initial business plan rather than defend it.

Do not automatically agree with the original recommendations.

Identify weaknesses, unsupported assumptions, missing information, and potential failure points.

## Critical Review Framework

### 1. Business Model Scalability

Evaluate:

- Is revenue overly dependent on the founder's working hours?
- Can the business operate without the founder's direct involvement?
- Are there opportunities to build reusable digital assets?
- Can the revenue model be expanded beyond one-time services?

Propose alternatives where appropriate.

### 2. Financial Assumption Validation

Evaluate:

- Are revenue assumptions overly optimistic?
- Are operating costs incomplete?
- Are hidden costs missing?
- Are pricing assumptions supported by evidence?
- Is the break-even point realistic?

Where appropriate, create:

- Pessimistic scenario.
- Base scenario.
- Optimistic scenario.

Clearly label all estimated figures and assumptions.

Never present scenario projections as actual financial results.

### 3. Pricing and Product Strategy

Evaluate whether the proposed pricing structure supports the business model.

Consider a product ladder:

- Free products or content.
- Low-cost products.
- Core products or services.
- Premium offerings.

Explain how each level contributes to customer acquisition, conversion, and revenue generation.

### 4. Legal and Regulatory Risks

Identify possible legal, licensing, employment, and regulatory issues.

For every issue:

- Explain why it matters.
- Identify what information is missing.
- Specify what requires professional verification.
- Recommend the appropriate type of professional consultation.

Do not present unverified legal interpretations as definitive advice.

### 5. Marketing Funnel

Evaluate the customer acquisition process.

Where relevant, structure the funnel as:

- TOFU: Awareness and traffic.
- MOFU: Consideration and lead nurturing.
- BOFU: Conversion.
- Retention: Repeat purchases and customer relationships.

Identify measurable KPIs, but distinguish proposed targets from actual performance.

### 6. Risk Management

Create a risk register containing:

| Risk | Impact | Likelihood | Proposed Mitigation | Validation Required |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Include financial, operational, legal, competitive, and organizational risks where relevant.

### 7. KPI Framework

Recommend a KPI framework covering:

- Traffic.
- Lead generation.
- Conversion.
- Revenue.
- Customer acquisition cost.
- Customer lifetime value.
- Customer satisfaction.

Do not invent historical performance data.

Clearly label suggested KPIs and target values as proposed targets.

---

# Part 3 — Final Output

Rebuild the business plan using the critical review.

Include:

1. Executive Summary.
2. Business Positioning and Model.
3. Financial Model and Scenario Analysis.
4. Human Resources and Compliance.
5. Digital Marketing Funnel.
6. Pricing Strategy.
7. Risk Management.
8. Implementation Roadmap.
9. KPI Framework.

At the end, provide:

## A. Key Changes

Compare the initial plan with the revised plan.

## B. Unresolved Questions

List all critical questions that still require human investigation.

## C. Human Decision Points

Identify decisions that must be made by the business owner rather than AI.

## D. Validation Checklist

Identify the financial, legal, market, and operational information that must be independently verified before implementation.

## E. Evidence Classification

Label each important statement as one of:

- USER-PROVIDED
- AI-GENERATED
- ASSUMPTION
- EXTERNALLY-VERIFIED
- REQUIRES-HUMAN-VALIDATION

## Final Instruction

A well-written business plan is not proof of a viable business.

Prioritize analytical honesty over persuasive writing.

Your goal is to help the user make better decisions, not to convince the user to pursue the business idea.
